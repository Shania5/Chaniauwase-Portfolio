01 / Design intent

A manageable mechanism.
A complete design problem.

I chose a personal gearbox project to work through the relationships between speed, torque, gear size, and the loads carried by the shafts and bearings.

The selected architecture divides the 6:1 reduction into 2:1 and 3:1 stages. Compared with one 20-tooth pinion driving a 120-tooth gear, this distributes the reduction between smaller gear pairs. The final housing envelope still depends on shaft arrangement and bearing placement.

Spur gears keep the initial geometry straightforward. The intermediate shaft carries both the first-stage driven gear and the second-stage pinion, connecting the two reductions.

The maths has a head start. The gears are waiting for their CAD debut.

02 / Speed, torque, and power

The numbers behind the reduction.

Design inputs: 1,800 RPM and 0.5 N·m at the input shaft. Overall efficiency is assumed to be 90% for an initial output estimate; it has not been measured.

ShaftSpeedIdeal torque, ignoring losses
Input1,800 RPM0.5 N·m

Overall ratio = (40 / 20) × (60 / 20) = 6
Angular speed = 2π × RPM / 60
Input power = 0.5 × 188.50 ≈ 94.25 W
Estimated output power = 0.90 × 94.25 ≈ 84.82 W
Estimated output torque = 84.82 / 31.42 ≈ 2.70 N·m

The 1.0 N·m intermediate torque is an ideal reference. Actual intermediate torque depends on first-stage losses, which have not been separately specified.

04 / Loads and shaft sizing

A first pass at the forces.

Preliminary shaft comparison +

A torsion-only comparison at the ideal output torque of 3.0 N·m gave approximately 70.7 MPa maximum shear stress for a 6 mm solid circular shaft and 29.8 MPa for an 8 mm shaft.

Maximum torsional shear stress τ = 16T / (πd³)

This supported carrying 8 mm forward as a preliminary shaft diameter, not a validated selection. Bearing spacing and gear positions are needed to calculate bending and combined loading. Material, fatigue, stress concentrations, keyways, deflection, and a safety factor must also be checked before final sizing.

What this project demonstrates

Following a requirement through the system.

A reduction ratio leads to tooth counts. Tooth counts and module lead to gear dimensions. Torque and gear dimensions lead to forces, which inform the shafts and bearings. The next stage is making those decisions work together in an assembly.

All dimensions and performance values on this page are preliminary calculations or assumptions. The design has not yet been manufactured or tested.
